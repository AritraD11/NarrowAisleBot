# The BNO055 fixes heading, not translation

Wire the BNO055 with five conductors (VIN to the ESP32's 3V3 pin, GND, SDA to GPIO14, SCL to GPIO13, RST to GPIO27 driven open-drain). Then run the bus at 100 kHz, put the chip in IMU mode (IMUPLUS, 0x08) instead of the NDOF mode Adafruit's `begin()` picks by default, read it from its own task on core 0, and fuse only its yaw rate into a robot_localization EKF next to the wheels' vx, vy and vyaw. That's most of the job, and it aims at the one error NarrowAisleBot has in bulk. Wheel odometry invents **0.37 to 0.60° of yaw per metre** while the chassis physically comes back square `[REPO]`. With slam_toolbox's scan matching off, whatever heading odometry believes goes into the map and into Nav2's goal check one for one `[SRC]`. A gyro removes most of that error. Whether it gets the G9 run under 1° is still open. Angle random walk alone costs about **0.3 to 0.4° over 8 to 15 minutes** `[INF from DS-snip]`, and the result depends on two numbers nobody has measured for this part: the bias left after the chip's own offset tracking, and the gyro's scale error. A 15-minute static log and ten slow full turns would settle both. The limits are just as firm. The IMU can't see translational slip at constant velocity, so the 1.245 m reading on a 1.00 m strafe and the 0.80 versus 0.92 lateral scale stay exactly where they are. It can't give absolute heading indoors next to a 30 A motor bus, and accelerometer dead reckoning loses to the wheels within a second. One warning applies to every datasheet figure here: **the Bosch BNO055 datasheet PDF couldn't be opened from this environment**, because the proxy blocked bosch-sensortec.com and every mirror tried. Those numbers came second-hand, through search-engine snippets, forum quotes or driver comments, and each one has to be checked against the rev 1.8 PDF before it goes into a gate document.

Each number below carries its evidence grade in brackets. These are the project's six grades, plus two more the sources needed:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[DS-snip]` | Bosch datasheet value seen only second-hand (search snippet, forum quote, driver comment). Check against the PDF |
| `[SRC]` | Source code read directly, file and line |
| `[PEER]` | Peer-reviewed measurement |
| `[COMM]` | Community report, including individuals' informal bench measurements |
| `[REPO]` | This repository's own measurement or files |
| `[INF]` | Inference or arithmetic from graded items |
| `[DOC]` | Official vendor or package documentation, including Adafruit's Eagle schematics read net by net |
| `[PRE]` | Preprint, not peer reviewed |

## Five wires, with GPIO14 and GPIO13 on the bus and GPIO27 on reset

The repo already reserved this I2C pair. `Master_Reference.md` §4.5 sets aside GPIO14 and GPIO13 with `Wire.begin(G14,G13)` and calls GPIO27 the best spare `[REPO]` ([Master_Reference §4.5](../docs/Master_Reference.md)). Neither 14 nor 13 is a strapping pin. The ESP32's strapping pins are 0, 2, 5, 12 and 15, and 6 to 11 belong to flash `[DOC]` ([ESP-IDF GPIO reference](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/release/v5.5/docs/en/api-reference/peripherals/gpio/esp32.inc)). Both are JTAG pins, which this firmware doesn't use. The plan below is for an Adafruit 2472 or 4646. It comes from Adafruit's own Eagle schematics, parsed net by net `[DOC]` ([2472 schematic](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit-BNO055-Breakout-PCB/blob/master/Adafruit%20BNO055.sch), [4646 schematic](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit-BNO055-Breakout-PCB/blob/master/Adafruit%20BNO055%20STEMMA%20QT.sch)).

| Breakout pin | ESP32 connection | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| VIN | 3V3 pin | On the 2472, VIN feeds the MIC5225-3.3 regulator and also the header-side 10 kΩ SDA/SCL pull-ups. With 5 V on VIN, GPIO13 and GPIO14 would be pulled to 5 V through 10 kΩ `[DOC, INF]` |
| GND | GND pin beside 3V3 | Star ground at the ESP32, never at a motor-driver ground |
| SDA | GPIO14 | Reserved pair, not a strapping pin |
| SCL | GPIO13 | Reserved pair. The BNO055 stretches this line |
| RST | GPIO27 as `OUTPUT_OPEN_DRAIN`, idling high-Z | The only recovery for a chip holding SDA low. On Adafruit boards a 1N4148 diode isolates it, so it can only pull low |
| INT | Not connected | No safe GPIO is left, and the data-ready behaviour isn't dependable |
| ADR, PS0, PS1 | Not connected on Adafruit boards; tie to GND on clones | 10 kΩ pull-downs already select I2C at address 0x28 |
| 3Vo | Not connected | Tying the onboard LDO output to the ESP32's 3V3 would back-feed it |

GPIO27 goes to reset because nothing else is left. Count the pins the drive firmware uses plus the reserved I2C pair (1, 3, 4, 13, 14, 16 to 19, 21 to 23, 25, 26, 32 to 36 and 39). GPIO27 is then the only pin that's neither strapping nor flash `[INF]`, so RST and INT compete for it. Reset wins easily. A software reset through `SYS_TRIGGER` needs a working bus, and the failure you most need to recover from is a chip whose I2C engine has wedged with SDA low. INT is far less useful. Bosch's reference driver defines interrupt bits only for motion events: gyro any-motion, gyro high-rate, accel high-g, any-motion and no-motion. A search of `bno055.h` and `bno055.c` finds no data-ready bit at all `[SRC]` ([bno055.h L1505-1525](https://github.com/BoschSensortec/BNO055_driver/blob/master/bno055.h#L1505-L1525)). A Bosch responder says data-ready can be enabled, but users report it never firing on their firmware `[COMM]` ([Bosch community](https://community.bosch-sensortec.com/t5/MEMS-sensors-forum/BNO055-Fusion-Interrupt/m-p/22747)). If interrupt status is ever wanted, poll INT_STA (0x37) over I2C.

Driving GPIO27 open-drain matters for a quiet reason. While the pin floats, the board's own 10 kΩ pull-up holds nRESET high, so an ESP32 reboot can't reset the IMU by accident. The firmware should still pulse reset deliberately at every ESP32 boot. A brownout, the standing suspect behind the ESP32's silent resets `[REPO]` ([Hardware_Roadmap](../docs/Hardware_Roadmap.md)), leaves the BNO055 in an unknown state. Pull the pin low for about 10 ms, a placeholder because the datasheet minimum couldn't be read. Then wait. The datasheet gives 650 ms from reset to config mode `[DS-snip]` ([Bosch datasheet](https://www.bosch-sensortec.com/media/boschsensortec/downloads/datasheets/bst-bno055-ds000.pdf)), and Adafruit's `begin()` polls for up to 850 ms because the chip "can take 850 ms to boot" `[SRC]` ([Adafruit_BNO055.cpp L84-96](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit_BNO055/blob/master/Adafruit_BNO055.cpp#L84-L96)). A reset clears calibration, so offsets come back from NVS afterwards. One loose end remains. Pulling the diode-isolated pin to 0 V leaves nRESET about one diode drop above ground. That's Adafruit's intended design, but the BNO055's nRESET threshold couldn't be confirmed without the datasheet.

The most dangerous single line of firmware is an argument-less `Wire.begin()`. On the plain `esp32` variant the default pins are SDA 21 and SCL 22 `[SRC]` ([pins_arduino.h L9-10](https://github.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/blob/3.3.12/variants/esp32/pins_arduino.h#L9-L10)). On this robot GPIO21 is the rear-right direction pin and GPIO22 is the rear-left PWM `[REPO]` ([aislebot_esp32.ino L96-97](../aislebot_esp32.ino)). `i2cInit()` calls `perimanClearPinBus()` on both pins, which detaches whatever peripheral currently owns them `[SRC]` ([esp32-hal-i2c-ng.c L107](https://github.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/blob/3.3.12/cores/esp32/esp32-hal-i2c-ng.c#L107)). The IMU would silently take a motor's LEDC channel. Adafruit_BusIO's `begin()` does call `Wire.begin()` without pins `[SRC]` ([Adafruit_I2CDevice.cpp L30-31](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit_BusIO/blob/master/Adafruit_I2CDevice.cpp#L30-L31)). If the bus is already running, though, the core just logs a warning and keeps the existing pins `[SRC]` ([Wire.cpp L299-302](https://github.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/blob/3.3.12/libraries/Wire/src/Wire.cpp#L299-L302)). So `Wire.begin(14, 13, 100000)` has to run first, every time.

Two board details catch people out. The 4646 STEMMA QT revision puts SCL on header pin 4 and SDA on pin 5, the reverse of the 2472 `[DOC]`, so wire by the silkscreen and never by pin position. Clones (GY-BNO055, CJMCU-055) vary more. On these, PS0 and PS1 are solder pads that must be bridged to GND for I2C, and some ship open. Their pull-ups may go to VIN, users have needed extra 4.7 kΩ pull-ups, and some boards reportedly carry no Bosch die at all `[COMM]` ([Arduino forum](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/gy-bno055-issues/551978)). The vendor of this robot's board still isn't known. If it's an unknown-vendor board, meter SDA and SCL to VIN versus 3V3 before powering it. On first boot, log CHIP_ID (expect 0xA0) and SW_REV_ID (0x04 to 0x05).

### 100 kHz on stock pull-ups, a short cable, and a core no older than 3.2

Run the bus at 100 kHz and leave it there. The ESP32 supports 100 and 400 kHz, and Espressif recommends 2 to 5 kΩ pull-ups `[DOC]` ([ESP-IDF I2C docs](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/release/v5.5/docs/en/api-reference/peripherals/i2c.rst)). Adafruit's boards fit 10 kΩ, and arduino-esp32 3.3.12 also turns on the weak internal pull-ups `[SRC]` ([esp32-hal-i2c-ng.c L98-131](https://github.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/blob/3.3.12/cores/esp32/esp32-hal-i2c-ng.c#L98-L131)). That gives an effective 8 to 10 kΩ. With that, the standard rise-time limits allow about **130 pF of bus capacitance at 100 kHz but only about 39 pF at 400 kHz** `[INF]`. The 1000 ns and 300 ns limits are UM10204 values quoted from memory, not fetched. 39 pF is roughly what the pins and a short wire already use. Community experience agrees. Adafruit runs this chip at 100 kHz, and the micropython-bno055 author found 400 kHz unreliable on weak pull-ups `[COMM]` ([micropython-bno055 README §2](https://github.com/micropython-IMU/micropython-bno055/blob/master/README.md#23-pico-issue)). One ESP32 user needed about 1.8 kΩ effective before the chip was detected at all `[COMM]` ([esp32.com](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=27307)). The faster clock buys nothing anyway. A 32-byte burst at 100 kHz takes about 3.2 ms of a 10 ms period on core 0, and the CPU is free during the transfer. If 400 kHz is ever needed, add roughly 3.3 kΩ to 3V3 at the ESP32 end and look at the edges on a scope first.

Keep the cable under about 30 cm. Twist SDA and SCL each with a ground, or lay a ribbon out GND-SDA-GND-SCL-VIN. Keep it off the 5 kHz PWM ribbon and the MDD20A motor leads, and cross them at right angles `[INF]`. The core's 7-cycle glitch filter only rejects very short spikes and is no substitute for layout. The board should also sit well away from the 30 A conductors and the 24 V boost converter. The magnetic reason mostly goes away once the magnetometer is off. The thermal reason doesn't, as the error budget later shows.

Clock stretching is why ESP32 plus BNO055 has a bad reputation, and most of that reputation belongs to old cores. The chip holds SCL low for something like 500 µs. The micropython-bno055 author measured "a maximum of about 500μs" and found no Bosch documentation of it `[COMM]` ([README §2.2](https://github.com/micropython-IMU/micropython-bno055/blob/master/README.md#22-clock-stretching)). Adafruit's driver comments "500us or more" `[SRC]` ([Adafruit_BNO055.cpp L48-51](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit_BNO055/blob/master/Adafruit_BNO055.cpp#L48-L51)). Another user measured about 180 µs, which Bosch staff called normal `[COMM]` ([Bosch community](https://community.bosch-sensortec.com/mems-sensors-forum-jrmujtaw/post/bno055-long-i2c-clock-stretching-vh3nGpxZKy0YOAQ)). The legacy ESP-IDF default timeout of 32000 APB cycles works out to **400 µs**, shorter than the stretch `[SRC, INF]`, which explains the old failure reports. arduino-esp32 has set the 20-bit hardware maximum (about 13 ms) since 2.0.2. From 3.2.0 on, the new `i2c_master` driver waits 2000 µs on the ESP32 `[SRC]` ([i2c_ll.h L73](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/release/v5.5/components/hal/esp32/include/hal/i2c_ll.h#L73)).

One conflict stays unresolved. Adafruit's troublesome-chips page, seen only as a snippet, says the ESP32 works poorly with this chip before ESP-IDF 5.3.2 `[DOC, snippet only]` ([Adafruit learn](https://learn.adafruit.com/i2c-addresses/troublesome-chips)). That would include cores 3.0 and 3.1, even though their legacy path sets the maximum timeout. Pin the core at 3.3.12 (ESP-IDF 5.5) and verify on the bench. ESP-IDF also warns that a slave can stretch for as long as 12 ms `[DOC]`, which a 2000 µs wait wouldn't survive, and the Arduino API has no way to raise `scl_wait_us`. If stretch timeouts appear in the error counter, the fix is to call ESP-IDF's `i2c_master` directly or patch the core `[INF]`. Set `Wire.setTimeOut(10)` (capital O; the default is 50 ms `[SRC]`) so a failed read costs one IMU period rather than five `[INF]`. ESP-IDF 5.5 clears a stuck bus by itself. After a timeout, the next transaction toggles SCL up to nine times and then forms a STOP `[SRC]` ([i2c_master.c L62-88](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/release/v5.5/components/esp_driver_i2c/i2c_master.c#L62-L88)). Adafruit's library has no recovery of its own. `getVector()` and `getQuat()` ignore the read's return value and convert a zeroed buffer. A failed read therefore returns all zeros, including an invalid zero quaternion, and `begin()` spins on CHIP_ID with no bound `[SRC]` ([Adafruit_BNO055.cpp L401-488](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit_BNO055/blob/master/Adafruit_BNO055.cpp#L401-L488)).

### Thirteen milliamps from the 3V3 pin

Power is the easy part. The chip runs on 2.4 to 3.6 V and draws about **12.9 mA in NDOF** `[DS-snip, via a third-party summary]` ([wolles-elektronikkiste](https://wolles-elektronikkiste.de/en/bno055-9-dof-sensor)). Users measure around 12 mA `[COMM]` ([Bosch community](https://community.bosch-sensortec.com/t5/MEMS-sensors-forum/High-current-consumption-of-BNO055/m-p/8364)). That's small change on a devkit 3V3 rail that stopped feeding a WiFi radio when firmware v3.0 removed it `[REPO]`. A 2020 thread titled "BNO055 draws 70mA in config mode" exists, but only its title was seen `[COMM]`. Feeding VIN from 3V3 through the breakout's own LDO and its 10 µF capacitors gives the chip a small second filter stage. The real supply risk sits upstream, in the Pi's USB 5 V and in PWM ground transients `[REPO]` ([Master_Reference](../docs/Master_Reference.md)). The repo's planned VBUS cut fixes that for the ESP32 and the IMU together. No separate supply is needed.

## Firmware reads one checked 32-byte burst every 10 ms on core 0

For the 100 Hz path, skip the libraries and read registers directly. The deployed drive firmware needs "no libraries beyond the ESP32 core" `[REPO]` ([aislebot_esp32.ino L76-77](../aislebot_esp32.ino)). The Adafruit library would add two dependencies. It also brings silent zeros on failure, an unbounded loop in `begin()`, `double` math and fixed 25 to 30 ms delays inside mode changes `[SRC]`. Bosch's own C driver does return error codes. It needs function-pointer glue, though, and waits 600 ms on every switch into a fusion mode `[SRC]` ([bno055.h L847-848](https://github.com/BoschSensortec/BNO055_driver/blob/master/bno055.h#L847-L848)). A direct implementation is perhaps 40 to 60 lines, using register constants copied from Bosch's header. Keep the Adafruit library for one-off bench sketches, where its offset and remap helpers save time. Don't port the never-run mini firmware either. It polls `getEvent` twice every 1 ms inside the Comms task, which is about 1 kHz of I2C traffic blocking serial parsing. It reads Euler yaw in degrees and has no error handling `[REPO]` ([narrowaislebot_mini_esp32.ino L617-625](../firmware/narrowaislebot_mini_esp32.ino)). It also calls `bno.begin()` with no argument, which selects NDOF `[SRC]` ([Adafruit_BNO055.h](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit_BNO055)).

### IMU mode, because each NDOF failure mode breaks a 1° gate by itself

The BNO055 has five fusion modes. Only COMPASS, NDOF_FMC_OFF and NDOF give an absolute, magnetic-north heading. IMUPLUS (0x08) fuses only the accelerometer and gyro, so its heading is relative to power-on `[DS-snip, SRC]` ([micropython-bno055 README](https://github.com/micropython-IMU/micropython-bno055), [BNO055_driver](https://github.com/BoschSensortec/BNO055_driver)). NDOF has a poor record for this job. In Kris Winer's 2015 bench test, the chip measured **87.1 ± 1.1° per 90° turn and lost 10° over a 360° rotation**. Heading also jumped 20 to 40° after the board was handled, which he blamed on autocalibration constantly rewriting the bias registers `[COMM, measured]` ([Winer wiki](https://github.com/kriswiner/MPU6050/wiki/Hardware-Sensor-Fusion-Solutions)). Forum reports describe jitter and drift of up to 10° once calibration reaches 3/3, and ±20 to 30° errors after power cycles `[COMM]` ([Bosch community](https://community.bosch-sensortec.com/t5/MEMS-sensors-forum/BNO055-unreliable-calibration-amp-heading-readings/td-p/57796/page/4)). NDOF keeps recalibrating the magnetometer, with no way to stop it `[COMM]` ([Experimental Avionics](https://experimentalavionics.com/bno055-disappointments/)). The physics on this robot is worse than on a bench. A straight conductor carrying 30 A produces about **120 µT at 5 cm and 30 µT at 20 cm** `[INF]`. That's comparable to Earth's field or larger, and it changes with motor current, so no calibration can absorb it. NDOF_FMC_OFF only slows the magnetometer calibration. The magnetometer still steers the heading.

The strongest case for NDOF comes from Peter Hinch's test. A field that turned a hiker's compass by 90° moved a calibrated BNO055's heading by at most 2° `[COMM]` ([micropython-bno055 README](https://github.com/micropython-IMU/micropython-bno055)). So the fusion rejects transient disturbances well. But a current-dependent field that rides along with the robot isn't a transient, and neither is rack steel it drives past. IMUPLUS is the mode. Make it a runtime setting rather than a constant, so a measured NDOF comparison stays possible later, as `Year2_Autonomy_Research.md` §6.2 already asks `[REPO]`. The fallback is the raw AMG mode (0x07), for the case where on-chip offset tracking turns out to step the heading mid-run. Raw modes let the host own bias and choose gyro range and bandwidth `[SRC]`. AMG costs more than the chip's compensation, though. One Bosch community user reports that non-fusion gyro data come out at 16.0 LSB/(°/s) while the underlying gyro is nearer 16.384 `[COMM]` ([Bosch community](https://community.bosch-sensortec.com/mems-sensors-forum-jrmujtaw/post/bno055-wrong-sensitivity-resolution-in-datasheet-pQrURrPcP5kORO7)). If that's true, it's a 2.4 % scale error, exactly what the ten-turn test exists to catch.

### Bounded init, explicit units, and a sign check before anything is fused

The init routine should replace Adafruit's `begin()`, and every step in it needs a bound `[INF]`. It starts with a RST pulse and polls CHIP_ID (0x00) for 0xA0 for no more than a second. Once the chip answers, it goes into CONFIG with normal power and page 0, and UNIT_SEL, AXIS_MAP_CONFIG (0x41) and AXIS_MAP_SIGN (0x42) get written explicitly. SYS_TRIGGER 0x80 follows only on boards that actually have the 32.768 kHz crystal. The 22 offset bytes from NVS go in last, if they exist, and only then does the firmware select IMUPLUS. The crystal bit comes after the remap because one user found the remap only took effect in that order `[COMM]` ([Adafruit forums](https://forums.adafruit.com/viewtopic.php?t=78161)). The sources disagree on mode-switch timing. A forum quote of the datasheet's Table 3-6 gives 7 ms out of CONFIG `[DS-snip]`, Bosch's own API waits 600 ms `[SRC]`, and Adafruit's `setMode()` waits 30 ms `[SRC]`. Bosch staff say the 7 ms covers only the internal MCU and that the sensors need longer ([Bosch community](https://community.bosch-sensortec.com/t5/MEMS-sensors-forum/BNO055-changing-mode-switching-delay/m-p/20855)). Use 600 ms and treat output inside that window as invalid.

Write UNIT_SEL (0x3B) rather than trusting the power-on defaults. Adafruit's `begin()` never sets it, because the write is commented out `[SRC]` ([Adafruit_BNO055.cpp L125-142](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit_BNO055/blob/master/Adafruit_BNO055.cpp#L125-L142)). Bosch's scale constants are 16 LSB per °/s or 900 per rad/s for the gyro, 100 LSB per m/s² for the accelerometer, and 2¹⁴ = 16384 LSB per unit for the quaternion `[SRC]` ([bno055.h L823-831](https://github.com/BoschSensortec/BNO055_driver/blob/master/bno055.h#L823-L831)). Anyone using the library should know about a version trap. `getEvent()` has returned gyro in rad/s only since v1.5.0 (commit `ed2d7ea`, August 2021) and returned deg/s before that. `getVector()` returns deg/s in every version `[SRC]` ([commit ed2d7ea](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit_BNO055/commit/ed2d7ea791ed2f8081820f2b876c698cb8bfba57)). So the mini firmware's comment that `gyr.gyro.z` is in rad/s only holds on 1.5.0 or later `[REPO]`.

Skip the Euler registers. Their heading increases clockwise from 0 to 360° `[DS-snip]` ([Bosch datasheet rev 1.4](https://www.mouser.com/datasheet/2/783/BST_BNO055_DS000_14-1221282.pdf)). Gyro z is positive counter-clockwise, as the FTC SDK's driver comments record before negating `[SRC]` ([OpenFTC Extracted-RC](https://github.com/OpenFTC/Extracted-RC)). Euler output also reportedly misbehaves at large combined tilt `[COMM]`. Use the quaternion and the gyro, and then prove the signs on the robot. Turn it counter-clockwise seen from above and confirm that gyro z is positive and quaternion yaw rises. Lay it level and confirm accel z reads about +9.8. Strafe right with a sharp start and look for a +X spike. None of the sources read states the quaternion's handedness relative to Euler heading, so these checks aren't optional.

### The task, the recovery ladder, calibration and the serial line

Give the IMU its own FreeRTOS task pinned to core 0 at priority 2, with a 4096-byte stack and a 10 ms `vTaskDelayUntil` period `[INF]`. That puts it above Comms (core 0, priority 1) and leaves the PID task alone on core 1 at priority 3 `[REPO]` ([aislebot_esp32.ino L1086-1128](../aislebot_esp32.ino)). Call `Wire.begin(14, 13, 100000)` inside that task, not in `setup()`. ESP-IDF allocates an interrupt on whichever core does the allocating `[DOC]` ([intr_alloc docs](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/release/v5.5/docs/en/api-reference/system/intr_alloc.rst)), and `setup()` runs in the Arduino loop task, typically on core 1 (that default wasn't verified for the installed board package). During a transfer the task blocks on the driver's semaphore `[SRC]`, so Comms keeps running. Samples pass through a sequence-counted struct under a `portMUX`, the same pattern as the existing `cmd_mux` `[REPO]`. The struct is about 48 bytes and copies in well under a microsecond, so the PID task on core 1 can read it as well once a yaw-rate loop exists.

Each cycle reads **one 32-byte burst from 0x08 to 0x27** with a repeated start. That covers accelerometer, magnetometer (zeros in IMUPLUS), gyro, Euler and quaternion in one transaction. The result is one time-coherent sample, one stretch window and one return code to check. At 100 kHz the frame is about 318 SCL cycles, or **3.18 ms**, plus up to half a millisecond of stretch `[INF]`. Read ACC_DATA at 0x08, which includes gravity, rather than the linear-acceleration block at 0x28, because REP-145 consumers expect +g at rest `[DOC]` ([REP-145](https://github.com/ros-infrastructure/rep/blob/master/rep-0145.rst)). Take `micros()` straight after the burst. Check that |q|² is close to 1 and the quaternion isn't all zero, and never publish a failed read as zeros. The chip's 100 Hz fusion clock isn't locked to the ESP32's timer, so expect the odd duplicated or skipped sample. Either detect duplicates by comparing raw bytes or tolerate them. Whether the chip latches multi-byte values during a burst is still an open datasheet question.

Failures climb a ladder `[INF]`. A single failed read is counted and skipped. Five in a row trigger `Wire.end()`, a manual nine-clock bus clear on GPIO13 and a fresh `Wire.begin()`. ESP-IDF's own clear runs as well, so this step is belt and braces. If reads still fail, pulse RST, wait about 850 ms, run the bounded init and restore the offsets. Past that point the firmware marks the IMU dead, sends a status line and retries every 5 s. All of this lives on core 0, so the wheel loop never waits on it. On the Pi, a stale IMU should make the EKF drop it. Stale means the same sample for more than about 100 ms, or an error state. A reset re-zeroes the IMUPLUS heading, and rate fusion is what lets the filter ride through one without a heading step.

CALIB_STAT (0x35) packs four 2-bit fields: SYS in bits 7-6, gyro in 5-4, accel in 3-2 and mag in 1-0 `[SRC]`. The gyro reaches 3 after a few seconds held still. The accelerometer wants six stable poses, with each axis perpendicular to the ground at least once `[DS-snip, via the datasheet text adapted in Hinch's README]`. In IMUPLUS, SYS isn't expected to reach 3. Adafruit's `isFullyCalibrated()` ignores it in that mode and requires only accel and gyro at 3 `[SRC]` ([Adafruit_BNO055.cpp](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit_BNO055/blob/master/Adafruit_BNO055.cpp#L788)). Offsets and radii live in 22 bytes at 0x55 to 0x6A, readable only in CONFIG after full calibration `[SRC]`. Restoring accelerometer offsets from NVS at every init is low risk. Restoring gyro offsets buys little. With a temperature coefficient of ±0.015 °/s/K typical `[DS-snip]`, a stored value goes stale after a few kelvin, and the chip relearns it at the next standstill anyway `[INF]`. Write NVS only on an explicit command, for example `<Q>` replying `[OK,IMU_CAL_SAVED]`, and accept it only while the motors are disabled. A flash write disables the caches and suspends every task on the other core, PID included `[DOC]` ([ESP-IDF flash concurrency](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/release/v5.5/docs/en/api-reference/peripherals/spi_flash/spi_flash_concurrency.rst)). Never save automatically.

The output line should be `[IMU]qw,qx,qy,qz,gx,gy,gz,ax,ay,az,t_us`. The bridge reads only fields 0 to 9 after `[IMU]` and ignores anything extra `[REPO]` ([esp32_bridge.py L320-346](../src/mecanum_robot/mecanum_robot/esp32_bridge.py)), so adding the ESP32 timestamp as an 11th field is backward-compatible. Format with `snprintf` into a stack buffer and send it with one `Serial.write`. `Print::printf` formats twice and calls `malloc` for any line of 64 characters or more `[SRC]` ([Print.cpp L46-71](https://github.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/blob/3.3.12/cores/esp32/Print.cpp#L46-L71)). Today's telemetry line is built from six separate Serial calls, and the PID task also prints trips, so lines can interleave `[REPO]`. Call `Serial.setTxBufferSize(1024)` before `Serial.begin()`. The default TX buffer is zero, and without one a write can block on the FIFO for about a millisecond `[SRC, INF]`. Match the decimals to the LSBs: `%.5f` for the quaternion, `%.4f` for rad/s and `%.2f` for m/s². An IMU line is 88 bytes. At 100 Hz, plus 20 Hz telemetry, that's about **10.5 kB/s, 11 % of the 921600-baud link** `[INF]`, so bandwidth isn't the constraint.

One trap sits on the Pi side. The bridge treats any comma-separated line with nine or more fields and a non-digit first field, other than `[IMU]`, as wheel speeds and publishes it on `/wheel_velocities_actual`. Before PR #29 the `[IMU]` line itself fell into that branch. A test caught it, it never happened on the robot, and the fix now handles `[IMU]` first `[REPO]` ([esp32_bridge.py L272-316](../src/mecanum_robot/mecanum_robot/esp32_bridge.py)). Any new status line has to stay at eight fields or fewer, for example `[IMUS,cal,i2c_err,reinit,state]`, or odometry will integrate it. Latency needs measuring too. The CP2102 on this devkit has no FTDI-style latency timer to tune: the string "latency" doesn't appear anywhere in Linux's `cp210x.c` `[SRC]` ([cp210x.c](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/drivers/usb/serial/cp210x.c#L308-L309)). The delay and jitter that arrival stamping adds therefore have to be measured. Log Pi arrival time minus `t_us` for ten minutes of driving.

## On the Pi, fuse yaw rate and leave nearly everything else out

The bridge needs four fixes before fusion means anything. It only publishes IMU data when `enable_imu` is true, and that defaults to False `[REPO]`. It never fills a covariance, so all three matrices go out as zeros. `Imu.msg` defines zeros as "covariance unknown" `[SRC]` ([Imu.msg](https://github.com/ros2/common_interfaces/blob/jazzy/sensor_msgs/msg/Imu.msg)), and robot_localization's `ekf.cpp` floors them at 1e-9, which makes the gyro look almost perfect `[SRC]` ([ekf.cpp](https://github.com/cra-ros-pkg/robot_localization/blob/jazzy-devel/src/ekf.cpp)). The package docs say the floor is 1e-6 and the code says 1e-9. Either value is wrong for this sensor. The bridge also stamps with Pi arrival time `[REPO]`, and it republishes every line as a String before it checks for `[IMU]`, which would add 100 String messages a second `[REPO]`.

Set the IMU yaw-rate variance from the datasheet's output noise of 0.1 to 0.3 °/s rms, which gives **3e-6 to 3e-5 (rad/s)²** `[DS-snip, INF]`. Put -1 in element 0 of anything the IMU doesn't provide, and move the `[IMU]` branch ahead of the raw republish. Later, replace arrival stamps with `t_us` plus an offset. Estimate the offset as the sliding-window minimum of arrival time minus device time, and unwrap `micros()` every 71.6 minutes `[INF]`. There's a naming mismatch as well. REP-145 reserves `imu/data_raw` for messages without orientation `[DOC]`, yet the bridge puts a quaternion on `/imu/data_raw`, and the drafted EKF subscribes to `imu/data` `[REPO]`. As drafted, the filter would quietly run on wheels alone. Run `ros2 topic info /imu/data_raw -v` and check that the EKF is listed as a subscriber, because robot_localization reports a missing topic only as an absence of data.

Start with a configuration that fuses wheel vx, vy and vyaw from `/wheel_odom` and only vyaw from the IMU. Use `two_d_mode: true`, `world_frame: odom`, 30 Hz and a 0.1 s sensor timeout `[INF]`. Each choice follows a documented rule. When odometry provides both pose and velocity, fuse the velocity, because fusing both feeds the filter duplicate information `[DOC]` ([configuring_robot_localization.rst](https://github.com/cra-ros-pkg/robot_localization/blob/jazzy-devel/doc/configuring_robot_localization.rst)). Two absolute yaw sources whose covariances don't overlap make the output "oscillate back and forth", so with N sources, N-1 must be differential `[DOC]`. Merrick and Nandikolla fused exactly this pair, wheel velocity plus IMU yaw rate `[PEER]` ([Sensors 26:5468](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26175468)). So did Ler et al., whose simulated IMU was modelled on this very part. They fused only velocities "to prevent injecting accumulated drift" `[PEER, simulated IMU]` ([Robotics 15:164](https://doi.org/10.3390/robotics15090164)). Fusing only the rate also keeps the EKF's yaw covariance honest. It grows slowly and without bound, which is the truth for a gyro heading. The EKF's 3D omnidirectional motion model rotates body-frame vx and vy into the world frame and never assumes +X is forward `[SRC]`. It handles lateral motion and this robot's +X-right base_link natively.

The drafted `ekf_params.yaml` needs rewriting, not patching. It fuses absolute wheel x, y and yaw alongside wheel velocities. It fuses IMU roll, pitch, yaw, all three rates and ax/ay, with `imu0_relative: false` and gravity removal on. It runs at 50 Hz and subscribes to the wrong topic. Its "tuned for mecanum" process noise is byte-for-byte the package template default `[REPO, SRC]` ([params/ekf.yaml](https://github.com/cra-ros-pkg/robot_localization/blob/jazzy-devel/params/ekf.yaml)). It's marked legacy and isn't launched `[REPO]` ([Firmware_Inventory §14](../docs/Firmware_Inventory.md)), so it hasn't hurt anything yet. Leave accelerations out entirely. The smoother limits the robot's real accelerations to 0.3 m/s². A 1° error in gravity removal leaves g·sin 1° ≈ 0.17 m/s² of false signal, more than half of that `[INF]`. Wheel vy deserves a variance near 2.4e-4 (m/s)², from the ±13 % lateral-scale spread at 0.12 m/s, against 1 to 2e-6 for vx `[INF]`. Today both are 0.01 `[REPO]`. Be clear about what that buys, though. With only one vy source, inflating its variance changes the reported uncertainty but not the mean pose. Only a per-floor lateral scale or scan matching moves the mean. Leave wheel vyaw at 0.03 (rad/s)², at least 10 to 100 times the IMU's, so the gyro dominates heading. Tune the process noise only after the first logged run, with `dynamic_process_noise_covariance: true` so standstills don't inflate it.

A second variant deserves an A/B on the G9 route. It adds the IMUPLUS yaw with `imu0_relative: true` and drops wheel orientation entirely `[INF]`. The chip integrates the gyro at 100 Hz internally, so heading survives a dropped or late serial line. A rate-only filter just holds the last vyaw between messages. The cost is a falsely small yaw covariance, plus double-counting if rate and yaw are both fused. On this stack that overconfidence is mostly cosmetic, because slam_toolbox, AMCL and MPPI don't read odometry covariance `[SRC, INF]`. Which variant wins depends on something nobody has confirmed: whether the gyro registers in IMUPLUS are offset-compensated like the fused orientation, or raw. If they're raw, rate-only fusion inherits a typical ±1 °/s offset `[DS-snip]` unless the host removes it. The 15-minute static log described in the last section answers this. A host-side bias estimate makes the rate-only setup safe either way. Take it at every standstill, when all four encoder deltas are zero and the commanded twist is zero, since any gyro output then is bias `[INF]`. Kiliç et al. used the same zero-angular-rate update in their slip detector `[PEER]` ([Field Robotics 2022](https://doi.org/10.55417/fr.2022054)).

Once the EKF starts, it has to be the only publisher of odom→base_link. `odometry_publisher` reads `publish_tf` only at construction, so switching it off is a launch-file change and a restart rather than a `ros2 param set` `[REPO, INF]`. slam_toolbox keeps publishing map→odom by TF lookup and needs no change `[SRC]` ([slam_toolbox_common.cpp](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/jazzy/src/slam_toolbox_common.cpp)). In the params file, Nav2's `odom_topic` entries for bt_navigator, controller_server and velocity_smoother point at `/wheel_odom` `[REPO]`. Repoint them to `/odometry/filtered` and confirm with `ros2 param get` on the live nodes, per the project's own rule. The dashboard's zero-point action must also publish to the EKF's `set_pose`, alongside `/odom/reset`, behind the same not-while-mapping refusal. Resetting either one under slam_toolbox causes the same origin jump `[SRC, REPO]`.

The frame check matters more than usual here. base_link is +X right, +Y forward, +Z up, which is right-handed `[REPO]`. The URDF's `imu_joint` sits at (0, 0, 0.05) with zero rotation, so the chip's own +X has to point to the robot's right `[REPO]` ([aislebot.urdf L341-345](../src/mecanum_robot/urdf/aislebot.urdf)). If the strafe test shows the chip's X pointing forward, put `rpy="0 0 1.5708"` on `imu_joint` (a +90° yaw carries the child's X onto base_link's +Y) or remap axes on the chip. Do one, never both, and never patch it in the bridge. robot_localization rotates rates and update masks by that transform, assumes ENU data, and "does NOT handle NED->ENU conversions" `[SRC]` ([ros_filter.cpp](https://github.com/cra-ros-pkg/robot_localization/blob/jazzy-devel/src/ros_filter.cpp)).

Timing is a smaller worry than it looks. Both streams share one serial link and one Pi clock, so most of the latency is common to both. Suppose the IMU and wheel lines are 20 ms apart. At the 0.30 rad/s yaw limit, that gives a 0.34° transient disagreement that doesn't accumulate `[INF]`. robot_localization applies an out-of-order measurement to the current state without rewinding, unless `smooth_lagged_data` is on `[SRC]` ([filter_base.cpp](https://github.com/cra-ros-pkg/robot_localization/blob/jazzy-devel/src/filter_base.cpp)). Host stamping can hide large offsets, though. One preprint found 0.5755 s between a USB BNO055 and a camera, discovered by calibration rather than assumed `[PRE]` ([arXiv 2603.26740](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2603.26740)). That's why `t_us` should go into the line now.

### With scan matching off, every degree the gyro saves lands in the map

With `use_scan_matching: false`, slam_toolbox's karto `Mapper::Process()` places each scan by applying the last odometric-to-corrected transform to the new odometric pose. It only calls `MatchScan`, adds graph vertices and edges, or attempts loop closure inside the scan-matching branch `[SRC]` ([Mapper.cpp L2716-2780](https://github.com/SteveMacenski/slam_toolbox/blob/jazzy/lib/karto_sdk/src/Mapper.cpp)). The live config's `do_loop_closing: true` therefore does nothing. The robot's own logs agree: two runs of 698 s and 18.5 m produced map→odom corrections of exactly zero `[REPO]` ([Research_Journal §17.56](../docs/Research_Journal.md)). Matching is off for good reason. In Stage G the wheels alone closed to 0.229 m, while wheels plus the scan-matching front end closed to 0.706 m `[REPO]` ([Phase2_Without_IMU §3](../docs/Phase2_Without_IMU.md)). So right now the EKF is the only thing that can correct heading in the map. Each degree the gyro removes takes away the "doubled wall after a turn" effect and sharpens Nav2's sense of where it is. It does nothing for lateral scale or LiDAR range noise.

This is also why the published dilution result doesn't apply yet, and the project's documents cite it too gently. Merrick and Nandikolla fused wheel speed with IMU yaw rate. Before SLAM, they cut raw-odometry APE RMSE by **about 61 to 75 % in translation and 65 to 77 % in rotation** `[PEER]` ([Sensors 26:5468](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26175468)). The translational range is the abstract's; the body text says 60 to 75 %. After SLAM, the change "did not consistently favor the improved or raw localization source". For SLAM Toolbox, the filtered odometry cut rotational error by 36.9 % and 24.0 % on two trajectories and **raised it by 22.6 % and 29.7 % on the other two**. Karto's translational error rose with EKF odometry on three of the four `[PEER]`. That pattern is inconsistent and sometimes worse, which is a different finding from "much smaller, algorithm-dependent gains", the wording in `Year2_Autonomy_Research.md` §6.1 ([Year2 §6.1](../docs/Year2_Autonomy_Research.md)). The study ran ROS 1 Melodic and slam_toolbox 1.1.6 with default parameters, so scan matching was on. It used one recorded bag run five times, and the authors themselves say those runs aren't independent samples `[PEER]`. For this robot their numbers are an order-of-magnitude expectation that gyro heading dominates. They're also a warning that G12, which re-enables scan matching, is a real A/B whose answer could go either way.

The other citation needs a sharper fix. `Phase2_Without_IMU.md` §6 says Galati and co-workers report "roughly 88 % heading-drift reduction" from fusion ([Phase2 §6](../docs/Phase2_Without_IMU.md)). They don't. Galati, Mantriota and Reina report "a reduction of about 77% and 88% of the position and orientation error" from **closed-loop heading control**, a PID fed by a Kalman-filtered Xsens MTi-300, compared with open-loop driving. The robot was a 200 kg industrial platform on four 280 mm mecanum wheels `[PEER]` ([Sci. Rep. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-24270-x)). It's a control result obtained with an industrial-grade IMU. It supports the ESP32 yaw loop discussed below, not EKF fusion with a consumer part.

Nav2 benefits through TF rather than through any topic. MPPI uses the odometry twist only to seed the first velocity of each rollout (`optimizer.cpp` L230, L327-331), and it ignores pose and covariance `[SRC]` ([optimizer.cpp](https://github.com/ros-navigation/navigation2/blob/jazzy/nav2_mppi_controller/src/optimizer.cpp)). The gain shows up in the critics and the goal checker, which compare against the odom-frame pose. With wheel-only odometry, believed heading can be 2 to 6° off after 5 to 10 m `[INF]`. That's more than the 0.05 rad (2.9°) yaw goal tolerance, so "goal reached" gets declared in a frame that's wrong by more than the tolerance. The aisle budget is tighter still. The padded footprint tolerates only **6.3° of yaw in a 0.60 m aisle**, and each degree swings the ends of the 1 m body by 8.7 mm. The wheel-only estimator uses that up in roughly 10 to 15 m `[REPO]` ([Year2 §1](../docs/Year2_Autonomy_Research.md)). Heading error also moves remembered obstacles. The local costmap lives in odom, so a 3.85° or 4.49° error shifts an obstacle seen 1 m away by 67 or 79 mm sideways, more than the 60 mm footprint padding `[INF]`. That makes the gyro a direct input to the requirement that the robot never reverses into something it saw earlier.

Some things don't change. The EKF does nothing for the controller loop, which runs at 5.5 to 8.9 Hz against the 20 Hz requested `[REPO]`. It also adds a node to a Pi that's already short of CPU, so run it at 30 Hz and check `top` before and after. In saved-map mode, AMCL's omni motion model scales rotation noise with alpha1 and alpha2 `[SRC]` ([omni_motion_model.cpp](https://github.com/ros-navigation/navigation2/blob/jazzy/nav2_amcl/src/motion_model/omni_motion_model.cpp)). Gyro heading justifies lowering them from 0.2, while alpha5 (strafe) stays as it is. Only do that as a measured A/B against G5's criteria. Ler et al. found that EKF-fused odometry with default AMCL parameters produced "substantially higher variance and lower tolerance compliance", even though odometric drift was lower `[PEER, simulated IMU]` ([Robotics 15:164](https://doi.org/10.3390/robotics15090164)).

## A yaw-rate loop on the ESP32 and a slip discriminator are the control payoff

Every mecanum heading controller found in the literature puts an IMU heading or yaw-rate loop outside the per-wheel speed loops and feeds its output through the inverse kinematics. The measured heading gains are large. Galati's closed-loop system finished with a **maximum angle deviation of 0.40° over a 10 m straight path** `[PEER]` ([Sci. Rep. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-24270-x)). Niu et al. measured yaw error over 3.5 m. In longitudinal travel it went from a range of -2.5° to 0° down to ±0.6°. When strafing it went from **-10.5° to 0° down to -1° to 0°** `[PEER]` ([Niu et al. 2019](https://consensus.app/papers/details/bb677b8425165c448706273e0e85f21a/?utm_source=claude_desktop)), which suggests strafing, the point of this robot, gains the most. A conference paper reports direction error on a circular path falling from 12 to 18° to 3 to 6° `[PEER, conference, uncited]` ([Taufiqurrahman et al. 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/bb3a38e21eb35ad5adf6aa0a67d7b6ba/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). An ADRC heading controller beat classical PD by 36 to 99 % `[PEER]` ([Carrillo et al. 2025](https://consensus.app/papers/details/82bbc4068bda507d9329e9761cab24b2/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). None of them reports loop bandwidth. The only published gains come without units or a sample rate, so nothing transfers. Tune from a measured step response.

On this robot the loop belongs on the ESP32, and it should track yaw rate rather than hold an absolute heading `[INF]`. It sits between the body-twist command and the inverse kinematics: ω′ = ω_cmd + Kp(ω_cmd - ω_gyro) + Ki∫(ω_cmd - ω_gyro)dt. When MPPI commands zero rotation, the integral term acts as heading hold. When MPPI commands a turn, the loop tracks the commanded rate. An absolute heading hold would fight MPPI's turns instead. The argument for the ESP32 is bandwidth. Nav2's controller runs at 5 to 9 Hz, so with the usual tenfold margin a loop closed there can't cross over much above 0.5 to 0.9 Hz. Roller shocks and single-wheel slip happen faster than that. The ESP32 samples at 100 Hz, the BNO055 fuses at 100 Hz, and the wheel plant's time constant is about 0.09 s `[REPO]`, so the outer loop can sit only three to five times below the wheel loop. Nobody has measured the wheel loop's closed-loop bandwidth, so do a yaw-rate step first. The bridge currently sends `<V,…>` wheel speeds. Either switch it to the `<B,vx,vy,wz>` command the firmware already accepts `[REPO]` ([Firmware_Inventory §10.1](../docs/Firmware_Inventory.md)), or recover ω_cmd from the four setpoints using the firmware's existing forward kinematics.

Do the EKF first and the ESP32 loop second, as separately measured steps. That's the order `Year2_Autonomy_Research.md` §6.2 already gives, and the project's isolate-before-tuning rule demands it. The EKF is cheaper, and it improves MPPI's tracking automatically, because MPPI already closes heading through odom. The ESP32 loop then adds disturbance rejection above about 1 Hz. Plan for one side effect. Once the ESP32 closes yaw on the gyro, the wheels will turn to cancel slip-induced yaw, and wheel-derived yaw then records that correction as if the body had rotated `[INF]`. From that point the EKF must take yaw rate from the gyro alone, or give wheel vyaw a hugely inflated variance.

Heading hold won't fix the strafe overshoot. Galati's 0.17 m over 10 m, with 0.40° of heading error, is 1.7 % of path. That's the same order as this robot's 1.1 to 1.5 % `[PEER, REPO]`, which shows a robot can hold heading perfectly and still overshoot or drift sideways. Heading still matters a great deal for cross-track error, which grows as L·sin δ. The 4.49° phantom yaw over a 5 m strafe would give 0.39 m of cross-track, 1° gives 87 mm, and Galati's 0.40° gives 35 mm `[INF]`. A 0.48 m padded footprint in a 0.6 to 0.8 m aisle leaves 60 to 160 mm per side. Holding heading to about 1° is what keeps a 5 m traverse inside the aisle.

A calibrated gyro meets Nav2's tolerance for in-place turns comfortably. It supports the 0.5° docking target in G13 but can't deliver it alone. Here's the budget for one 90° turn at the 0.30 rad/s limit, which takes 5.24 s `[INF]`:

| Gyro condition | Heading error after the turn |
|---|---|
| Uncompensated 1 °/s offset | 5.2°, fails everything |
| Bias removed, scale off by 1 % (datasheet typical) | 0.9°, passes 2.9° but not 0.5° |
| Bias removed, scale off by 3 % (datasheet max) | 2.7°, borderline against 2.9° |
| Scale calibrated to 0.1 %, residual bias 0.05 °/s | About 0.35°, inside 0.5° |

The sensitivity tolerance behind that table (±1 % typical, ±3 % maximum) was seen as a datasheet snippet in only one of the four research passes. The other passes found no tolerance figure at all `[DS-snip]` ([Bosch datasheet, Adafruit copy](https://cdn-shop.adafruit.com/datasheets/BST_BNO055_DS000_12.pdf)). Check it first. Two practical constraints decide where turns happen. Rotating in place needs about 1.06 m of clear diameter for the body and 1.22 m for the padded footprint `[INF]`. That's impossible in 0.6 and 0.8 m aisles and marginal at 1.1 m. Rotation Shim ([Nav2 docs](https://docs.nav2.org/configuration/packages/configuring-rotation-shim-controller.html)) and MPPI should finish heading changes before aisle entry. Inside the aisle, the job is holding heading while strafing. The end of a turn also drops into the 0.05 to 0.20 rad/s band where the Kstat feedforward fades out and stick-slip lives `[REPO]`. The stop decision should therefore use measured body yaw. Mamat et al.'s relay controller with a tolerance deadband is the published pattern for this stage `[PEER]` ([Sensors 26:5692](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26185692)). The 0.5° itself has to come from a dock detector, such as an AprilTag, an IR beacon or a LiDAR fit to the dock face. The gyro bridges the gaps between detections.

### The gyro separates lever-arm error from rigid slip, once its own scale is known

This is the IMU's most useful diagnostic job, and the asymmetric chassis makes it more useful still. The repo already proposes the test. If the wheel-to-gyro yaw ratio holds steady across turns, the error is scale or lever arm. If it jumps now and then, it's rigid slip `[REPO]` ([Year2 §6.2](../docs/Year2_Autonomy_Research.md)). Kiliç et al. built the published version. They compared gyro and wheel yaw rate to identify when the vehicle wasn't turning, and INS-derived velocity against wheel velocity to get a slip ratio. With only an IMU and encoders, they reached **better than 92 % slip-detection accuracy over about 150 m** `[PEER]` ([Field Robotics 2022](https://doi.org/10.55417/fr.2022054)). This is what each signal can see on this robot `[INF]`:

| Error class | Encoder slip residual s | Gyro vs wheel yaw rate | Accel vs d/dt(wheel v) |
|---|---|---|---|
| One wheel slipping | Yes, that's what s is for | Only the part that yaws the body | Only while accelerating |
| Lever-arm or yaw-scale error | No | Yes, as a constant ratio | No |
| Rigid slip with a yaw component | No | Yes | Weakly |
| Rigid translational slip at constant velocity | No | No | No |
| Translational scale (radius, 0.80/0.92 lateral, 1.245 m strafe) | No | Only if front and rear slip differently | In theory, during ramps |

Define ρ = Δψ_wheel / Δψ_gyro for each turn segment. If ρ is constant across speed, direction and surface, recalibrate K. If it shifts with the floor, the effective lever arm depends on surface, the rotational cousin of the 0.80 versus 0.92 lateral finding. Mostly stable with occasional jumps at accelerations or roller shocks points to intermittent rigid slip. A ρ that depends on turn direction points at the asymmetric chassis, or at gyro offset nobody removed. There's a catch. The gyro's own scale error is indistinguishable from a lever-arm error. At ±1 % typical to ±3 % maximum, N net revolutions pick up 3.6°·N to 10.8°·N of gyro-only error `[DS-snip, INF]`. That's the same size as the 3.85 to 4.49° phantom yaw under investigation. So the gyro's scale gets pinned first.

That fixes the order of calibration. Bias comes from stationary averaging at the start and at every stop. Scale comes from many slow full turns in both directions against an external reference. Ten turns is 3600°, so a 1° reference error gives 0.03 % scale accuracy. Two references would do: the floor-grout photogrammetry, already validated to within 1° `[REPO]` ([Hardware_Roadmap §1.1](../docs/Hardware_Roadmap.md)), or LiDAR scans of a static scene before and after the turns `[INF]`. With a calibrated gyro, the asymmetric geometry pays off. K_o,eff = r∫(ω_FR - ω_RL)dt / (2Δψ_gyro) and K_i,eff = r∫(ω_RR - ω_FL)dt / (2Δψ_gyro) can be identified separately, per surface `[INF]`. If their ratio isn't 0.5607/0.4907, the slip residual's 1.142656 coefficient carries a bias during rotation and needs updating. None of the work found has done this on an asymmetric mecanum chassis, so it's a small but real thesis contribution.

Use 180 to 360° turns, not 90° ones. Mamat et al. found error reduction weaker on 90° tests. Across 720 in-place rotation trials on three surfaces, their calibration cut angular error by **59.13 to 96.58 %** `[PEER]` ([Sensors 26:5692](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26185692)). The available excerpts don't name their IMU, so don't cite the paper as IMU-referenced until the full text has been read. Translational scales (wheel radius, lateral scale) need a distance reference and get nothing from the gyro, with one exception. Yaw measured during a commanded pure strafe reveals the difference between front and rear lateral slip. It can never reveal the common-mode overshoot.

Two numbers set the detector `[INF]`. Per-sample gyro noise is roughly 0.1 °/s (0.0017 rad/s). A 1 % yaw-scale error at 0.30 rad/s is only 0.003 rad/s, so scale error has to come from integrated angle over whole turns, never from per-sample residuals. Slip events of 0.02 rad/s or more that last 0.2 s or longer stand about ten times above the noise and can be flagged one by one. Set the real threshold at 5 to 10 times the measured stationary σ of ω_gyro - ω_wheel, the same way `wheel_forensics.py` derived its own 0.5 rad/s episode threshold. Align the signals first. Wheel speeds are EMA-filtered (α 0.4) on the ESP32 and reach the Pi at 20 Hz with arrival stamps `[REPO]`. Either run the gyro through the same filter or take the difference on the ESP32 at 100 Hz. Otherwise every acceleration transient turns into a false residual.

### The accelerometer is a second witness, not a safety layer

At 0.12 m/s a collision is a gentle event, and that limits what the accelerometer can do `[INF]`. Stopping within a compliance distance d needs a = v²/2d. That's 0.36 m/s² over 20 mm, 1.44 m/s² over 5 mm and 7.2 m/s² over 1 mm. Normal driving can reach about 0.91 m/s² (the 12 rad/s² wheel slew limit times the 0.0762 m radius). Rigid hits stand out above that, but soft ones (cardboard, cloth, a shin) don't, and mecanum roller vibration sits on top of everything. A bare g-threshold detector would miss exactly the cases that matter. The published fix combines acceleration with the motor command. Becker and Ebner's logistic regression caught **13 of 14 collisions with no false positives** on a held-out set `[PEER, conference]` ([Becker & Ebner 2019](https://doi.org/10.5220/0007768601670173)). Speleers and Ebner found that a frequency-based averaging filter beat simple thresholds `[PEER, conference]` ([IRC 2019](https://doi.org/10.1109/irc.2019.00088)). On the ESP32, that means testing a_meas - a_cmd, with a_cmd known from the slewed setpoints. Combine it with the PID's own residual: the integral term and PWM rising while wheel speed falls. Fusion modes fix the sensor configuration `[DOC]` ([Bosch datasheet rev 1.4](https://www.mouser.com/datasheet/2/783/BST_BNO055_DS000_14-1221282.pdf)), and the fused bandwidth wasn't retrieved, so short impacts may be smeared. Treat the accelerometer as corroboration behind the costmap, the planned ToF ring and the contact bumper `[REPO]`.

The cheaper accelerometer jobs are more dependable. Pitch and roll of the gravity vector, measured against a reference taken at boot, flag dock ramps and floor thresholds, so the slip detector can suppress false alarms there. The same reading catches a mount working loose and can trigger a hard stop at a tilt this tall, 0.36 m-wide chassis should never see. Pick that threshold from the geometry, since no published value exists for this class of robot, and keep `two_d_mode: true` regardless `[INF]`. Cross-checks at 100 Hz explain trips well. If someone pushes the robot while it's parked, the gyro or accelerometer shows motion even though the wheels are commanded to zero. Lifting it makes the wheels spin up with no body acceleration to match. The most useful case is stuck-but-spinning, where wheel speed is high, body acceleration and yaw rate sit near zero, and the PWM isn't saturated. The existing stall trip can't catch it, because that trip needs wheel speed below 0.15 rad/s `[REPO, INF]`. Log these cross-checks in the `[TRIP,…]` line.

Galati's floor classifier carries over directly. The power spectrum of vertical acceleration separated concrete from asphalt `[PEER]`. With frequency normalised by wheel speed, it should give a surface classifier that picks the per-surface lateral scale and K. A peak growing at the roller-pass frequency over weeks would be a wear signal, though the roller count hasn't been measured `[INF]`. Log TEMP (0x34) and CALIB_STAT with every run too. A drop in gyro status from 3 marks the moment the chip changed its offset estimate, and that's a candidate cause of any heading step.

## Under 1° is a bet on bias and scale, and translation stays out of reach

The gyro has to beat a well-measured baseline. On 3 September, odometry reported -3.85° of yaw over 723.8° of commanded rotation while the chassis physically ended within 0.03° of where it started. That was measured by floor-grout photogrammetry, validated to within 1° against a frame of known -28° rotation, with a noise floor near 0.18° `[REPO]` ([Research_Journal §17.55](../docs/Research_Journal.md)). The 12 m route gave -4.49° over 482 s and one full turn with the chassis physically square. Phantom rates on its two runs were 0.60 and 0.37 °/m, and an earlier 18 m run showed 10.53° `[REPO]` ([§17.56](../docs/Research_Journal.md)). Over 10 to 20 m, that extrapolates to roughly 4 to 12° of wheel-only error. Two explanations survive for it: a wheel-radius or encoder scale error, or rigid-consistent slip. The current sensors can't see either `[REPO]`. A gyro can see both.

Three floors set the gyro's error, and they grow differently `[INF from DS-snip]`. Angle random walk grows with the square root of time, from the 0.014 °/s/√Hz noise density, and nothing removes it. Residual bias grows linearly with time. Scale error grows with total absolute rotation, not with time or distance. For the two G9 routes:

| Term (assumed level) | 12 m route, 482 s, 364.5° | Two-circle route, about 216 s, 723.8° |
|---|---|---|
| Angle random walk, 0.014 °/s/√Hz `[DS-snip]` | 0.31° | 0.21° |
| Residual bias, 0.001 °/s `[INF]` | 0.48° | 0.22° |
| Scale error, 0.1 % `[INF]` | 0.36° | 0.72° |
| 0.1 K of warming after bias was learned, at 0.015 °/s/K `[DS-snip]` | 0.72° | 0.32° |

Combine the best-case terms as a root sum of squares and the 12 m route lands near **0.5 to 1.2°**, depending on how well bias and scale are controlled `[INF]`. That assumes the errors have independent signs. A straight sum of the same best-case terms is about 0.9°, which shows how thin the margin is. The two-circle route needs scale held to about 0.1 %, which takes a calibration, not luck. The temperature row is the uncomfortable one. Holding 20-minute error under 1° needs residual bias below about 0.00083 °/s (3 °/h). At the typical temperature coefficient, a 1 K change after the last bias estimate is 18 times that budget `[INF from DS-snip]`. The typical/maximum split on that coefficient was itself inferred by a search tool from a garbled table, so it's one more number to check in the PDF. Mount the board away from the drivers and the boost converter, and warm up for several minutes with the motors powered. Hold still until gyro status reads 3 just before the run, and build standstills into the route so the chip, and the host, can relearn bias.

How good is the bias suppression in practice? Nobody has published a controlled BNO055 IMUPLUS drift rate on a ground robot, so every comparator is indirect. The closest relative-heading product is the BNO085's game rotation vector. It's reported at **0.08 °/min on one indoor vehicle and 0.5 °/min** in another account `[COMM]` ([OVCS PR #157](https://github.com/open-vehicle-control-system/ovcs/pull/157), [AgOpenGPS](https://discourse.agopengps.com/t/bno085-or-bno080-imu/2993?page=2)). Over 8 minutes that would be 0.64 to 4° `[INF]`, from a different chip and not a BNO055 measurement. A Bosch staff answer says the BNO055's gyro is the BMI088's, with bias instability under 2 °/h. Adafruit and Kris Winer say it's a BMI055 `[COMM]` ([Bosch community](https://community.bosch-sensortec.com/t5/MEMS-sensors-forum/THE-bias-instability-of-BNO055/td-p/94733), [Adafruit](https://learn.adafruit.com/comparing-gyroscope-datasheets)). If a 2 °/h bias simply persisted, it would cost about 0.27° over 8 minutes. Unpinned forum anecdotes put IMU-mode drift at 4° over several hours `[COMM]`. A 2026 preprint ran an Allan variance on a 7-hour static BNO055 log, but its values couldn't be retrieved `[PRE]` ([arXiv 2603.26740](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2603.26740)). My read is this. The registered 0.3 to 1.0° prediction for G9 has its lower end sitting on the random-walk floor. Getting the 12 m route under 1° is plausible with the procedure above, and the two-circle route will fail without a scale calibration. Even the pessimistic case, 1 to 2° over 15 minutes, is a three- to six-fold improvement on 4 to 12°.

Two measurements settle all of this, and together they take an afternoon `[INF]`. The first is a 15-minute static log in IMUPLUS after warm-up, recording quaternion yaw, raw gyro z, TEMP and CALIB_STAT. It gives the bias slope and also answers whether the gyro registers are offset-compensated. The second is ten slow full turns each way against grout photogrammetry, which gives the scale. Write the predictions down before either run. One tempting autonomy gate needs correcting here. A check of |mean gz| < 0.002 °/s over a 10 s hold can't be resolved. Averaging white noise of 0.014 °/s/√Hz for 10 s leaves about 0.004 °/s of uncertainty in the mean `[INF]`. Use the 10 s hold as a gross check at three sigma, about 0.013 °/s, and take the fine number from the long log.

### What the IMU can't see

Accelerometer dead reckoning is out, and the arithmetic is brutal `[INF]`:

| Constant horizontal error | Velocity error at 1 s | Position error at 1 s | At 10 s | At 60 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1° tilt of mount or floor (0.171 m/s²) | 0.17 m/s | 0.086 m | 8.6 m | 308 m |
| 0.1° tilt (0.0171 m/s²) | 0.017 m/s | 0.009 m | 0.86 m | 31 m |
| 10 mg residual offset (0.098 m/s²) | 0.098 m/s | 0.049 m | 4.9 m | 176 m |

The robot covers 0.12 m in a second and 1.2 m in ten, so a 1° tilt produces velocity error larger than top speed within one second. Woodman's strapdown INS on an Xsens MTx exceeded 150 m of average position error after 60 s `[DOC, tech report]` ([Woodman 2007](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk//techreports/UCAM-CL-TR-696.pdf)). The deeper problem isn't drift. Rigid translational slip at constant velocity is invisible to the whole IMU. True body acceleration and yaw rate are both zero whatever the wheels are doing, so a steady slide looks exactly like perfect rolling. The start and stop transients that do carry information are buried in tilt and offset error at this robot's accelerations of 0.1 g or less `[INF]`. That's why the 1.245 m strafe, the 0.80/0.92 lateral scale and the forward scale belong to other sensors.

Absolute heading is out too. IMUPLUS heading is relative to power-on and goes back to zero on any reset. The magnetometer can't stand in indoors. Handheld e-compasses showed an average error of 45° from true direction across indoor locations `[DOC, tech report]` ([MIT Media Lab 2011](https://www.media.mit.edu/speech/papers/2011/positioning.systems.pdf)). In a lab with steel structures and equipment, the field peaked at 127 µT, 2.78 times the mean `[PRE]` ([arXiv 2510.10979](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.10979)). The robot also carries its own current-dependent field. Over long missions, absolute heading has to come from AMCL on a saved map, from the dock detector, or from scan matching once G12 settles whether it helps. The gyro only improves the prior those methods start from. This table maps each blind spot to what covers it:

| Blind spot | What covers it |
|---|---|
| Translational slip, lateral and forward scale | Optical flow (PAA5100JE or PMW3901, after measuring ground clearance), or a sprung undriven measuring wheel per axis. Passive orthogonal wheels gave "a mechanically independent displacement measurement source" on a mecanum platform `[PEER]` ([Appl. Sci. 16:6827](https://doi.org/10.3390/app16136827)). One review statement found a downward camera on an omni robot less accurate than local odometry `[PEER]` ([AMM 2014](https://doi.org/10.4028/www.scientific.net/amm.658.587)), so measure before committing |
| Absolute heading and position | AMCL on a saved map, the dock detector, scan matching after G12 |
| Soft-obstacle collision | ToF ring plus a contact bumper wired into the hardware E-stop `[REPO]` |

Finally, the part itself is near the end of its life. DigiKey lists it as "Not For New Designs" and Mouser as "NRND", though both still show stock ([DigiKey](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/bosch-sensortec/BNO055/6136301), [Mouser](https://www.mouser.com/ProductDetail/Bosch-Sensortec/BNO055?qs=QhAb4EtQfbV8Z2YmISucWw%3D%3D)). A Bosch moderator calls it discontinued and points to the BHI260AP `[COMM]`, while Hinch reports it still available as of May 2026 `[COMM]` ([micropython-bno055 README](https://github.com/micropython-IMU/micropython-bno055)). REV moved its FTC Control Hubs from the BNO055 to the BHI260AP in September 2022 `[DOC]` ([FTC docs](https://ftc-docs.firstinspires.org/en/latest/programming_resources/imu/imu.html)), so much of the FTC lore about BNO055 drift describes older hubs. For a Year 2 thesis robot with the board already in hand, none of this matters much. Plan any replacement around the BNO085 game rotation vector, or a raw gyro with host-side fusion. The core-0 task, the `[IMU]` line with `t_us` and the rate-only EKF would all carry over unchanged.

## Record G9 before G12, then let the leftover error pick the next sensor

Right now the BNO055 is worth more on NarrowAisleBot than it would be on most robots, for a reason that has nothing to do with the chip. Scan matching is off, so nothing else in the stack corrects heading, and the dilution Merrick and Nandikolla saw can't happen yet. That reverses the usual advice to expect little from wheel-plus-IMU fusion under SLAM, and it sets the order of work. Record G9 with the EKF before touching G12. Once scan matching is back on, the gyro's measurable contribution may shrink or change sign, and the clean before-and-after comparison will be gone.

The IMU also sharpens the next sensor decision instead of replacing it. Once the gyro has pinned heading and separated the two lever arms, whatever odometry error remains has to be translational, and that's an error the IMU has already been shown unable to see. The ten-turn scale test, the per-surface lever arms and the residual left after G9 together make a measured case for optical flow or a measuring wheel. That's a stronger argument for the thesis than any datasheet comparison.
