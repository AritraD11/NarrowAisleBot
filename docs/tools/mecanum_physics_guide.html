Omnidirectional locomotion · interactive

The wheel that drives sideways.

A mecanum wheel spins like any other — yet a robot on four of them can slide left, crab diagonally, and spin in place without ever turning a wheel to face where it's going. The trick is a ring of rollers set at 45°, and a little vector arithmetic. This guide takes you from that single idea to the working machine, the equations, and the hard reasons it still isn't on your car.

STATION 01The 45° trick

A normal wheel can only push one way.

Roll a normal wheel forward and the ground pushes the robot forward. Try to shove that robot sideways and the wheel refuses — it grips. That grip is static friction, and for a normal wheel it can point in almost any direction to resist sliding. Useful for not skidding; useless for choosing to go sideways.

A mecanum wheel breaks the grip in exactly one direction. Around its rim sit free-spinning rollers, each tilted 45°. The rollers happily roll along their own axis, so the wheel cannot push the ground in that 45° direction — it just slides. What's left is a single hard direction, perpendicular to the rollers, where the wheel still grips and pushes.

So when the wheel is driven, the ground reaction it produces doesn't point forward. It points at 45°. Spin the wheel the other way and the 45° force flips to the opposite diagonal. That diagonal force is the whole game.

STATION 02Where the force actually comes from
contact patchfriction, constrained to one axis

Yes — it's friction. But a leashed friction.

You had the right instinct: the propelling force is friction at the contact patch, same as any wheel. Nothing magnetic, nothing exotic. The motor applies a torque, the rim tries to shear the ground, and friction pushes back on the robot.

The mecanum twist is what the roller does to that friction. A free roller can only be dragged along its own rolling direction with almost no resistance. So any friction component along the roller simply spins the roller and vanishes — no force delivered. Only the component perpendicular to the roller survives as a real push.

That's why the net force is pinned to the 45° line: the roller acts as a one-way valve for friction. Drive hard, and the useful force is the driven force times cos 45° ≈ 0.71 — the rest is spent sliding the rollers. This 0.71 "diagonal tax" quietly shows up again in Station 07 as an efficiency cost.

STATION 03Four wheels, and the cancelling that makes it move

Four diagonal forces, added like arrows.

Put four mecanum wheels on a chassis in a mirror pattern — the rollers form an X seen from above. Each wheel still only makes a 45° force. But four 45° arrows, pointed by the roller handedness and scaled by each wheel's speed, add and cancel. Pick the four speeds right and the sideways parts survive while the forward parts cancel — pure strafe. Choose a preset and watch the four force arrows resolve into one motion of the whole body.

STATION 04The equations that run every mecanum robot

Inverse kinematics: motion in, four speeds out.

A controller thinks in body motion — forward vₓ, sideways v_y, and turn rate ωz. The wheels only understand their own spin. Inverse kinematics is the dictionary between them. For a standard mecanum layout with wheel radius r and a chassis of half-length lₓ and half-width l_y:

ω_FL = (1/r)·( vₓ − v_y − (lₓ+l_y)·ωz )
ω_FR = (1/r)·( vₓ + v_y + (lₓ+l_y)·ωz )
ω_RL = (1/r)·( vₓ + v_y − (lₓ+l_y)·ωz )
ω_RR = (1/r)·( vₓ − v_y + (lₓ+l_y)·ωz )

Read one line and the whole scheme falls out: +v_y (strafe) adds to the right wheels and subtracts from the left; ωz (turn) adds to one side and subtracts from the other. Drive the sliders and watch the four wheels — and the little robot — respond in real time.

When the wheels aren't in a rectangle

The textbook form above assumes all four wheels sit at the corners of a rectangle, so a single lever arm (lₓ+l_y) serves every wheel. The NarrowAisleBot in this repo deliberately breaks that — its outer pair sits farther out than its inner pair — so it needs two different lever arms, K_outer and K_inner, one per diagonal. Same physics, one term per wheel instead of one shared term. Half the historical bugs on that robot came from a control path quietly using a single symmetric K and erasing the geometry.

STATION 05Rollers, radius, and the ride you feel
rim profileripple —

Why the count of rollers changes the ride.

A mecanum wheel never rolls on a smooth circle. At any instant only one or two rollers touch the ground, and each roller is a short barrel, not an arc of the full rim. So the effective rolling radius rises and falls as rollers hand off — the wheel centre bobs up and down a little on every rotation. That bob is real: it's a measurable vertical vibration that grows with speed and load.67

More rollers means shorter hand-offs and a smaller bob — a smoother ride — but more parts, more bearings, more cost, and more places for grit to jam. Fewer rollers is cheaper and tougher but rougher. Barrel-shaped rollers are profiled precisely to keep the contact radius as constant as they can; it's a manufacturing art, not a given.

The slider shows the trade directly: watch the contact-radius ripple shrink as you add rollers, and grow as you drop them.

STATION 07The reasons it isn't on your car

Brilliant in a warehouse aisle. Absent on the road.

Mecanum thrives exactly where it was born — tight, flat, indoor spaces where sideways beats a three-point turn: warehouse AGVs, factory floors, wheelchairs, camera dollies, forklifts in narrow racking. Step outside those conditions and a stack of real penalties appears. None is fatal alone; together they keep mecanum a specialist tool, not a general one.

Each penalty below is an active drag on adoption — and, flipped around, an open invitation for the research in Station 08. Severity is relative to a plain differential-drive robot on the same job.

Slip & dead reckoninghigh

Those free rollers are always partly sliding — that's the point — so wheel-encoder odometry drifts fast. Estimated position wanders metres over a run unless corrected by an IMU, LiDAR, or fused filter.12 Getting reliable position is a whole sub-field: sensor fusion,3 explicit slip models,4 and online slip estimation and compensation.5

Vibration & noisemedium

The roller hand-off makes the hub bob every revolution — vertical vibration that worsens with speed and load, jars payloads, and radiates noise. Reducing it drives real design work on roller count, profile, and equivalent stiffness.67

Load & ground contacthigh

Only one roller per wheel carries the load at a time, on a tiny patch, so contact force swings wildly through a rotation8 and all four wheels must stay planted. On uneven ground a wheel lifts, the force balance breaks, and motion smears — which is why "omni on rough terrain" is its own research thread.1112

Efficiency taxmedium

The 45° diagonal means a chunk of every motor's effort is spent sliding rollers, not moving the robot — the cos 45° tax from Station 02. Strafing can draw far more current than driving straight for the same speed, shortening range on battery platforms.

Wear, cost & debrismedium

Dozens of small rollers and bearings per robot are dozens of wear points. They're intolerant of grit, water, and rough or soft floors — a pebble in a roller is a dead axis. More parts, more maintenance, higher cost than a plain wheel.

Payload ceilingcontext

Small point contacts limit how much weight the rollers take before deforming or stalling — which is why heavy-duty omnidirectional platforms are a deliberate design specialty, not an off-the-shelf scale-up.910

STATION 08What engineers are actively trying to fix

Turn every limitation above into a research programme.

Beat the slipactive

Fuse wheel odometry with IMUs, LiDAR, and vision through Kalman/EKF filters;3 build explicit friction-and-slip dynamics into the controller instead of pretending contact is perfect;4 and estimate slip online to compensate it live.5 The goal: trust the robot's own sense of where it is.

Kill the vibrationactive

Optimise roller geometry and hub stiffness so the contact radius barely ripples — modelling the wheel as an equivalent spring and tuning roller count, overlap, and profile to flatten the bob.67

Adapt to terrainactive

Suspension and compliance that keep all wheels loaded on bumps, plus kinematic models built for uneven ground rather than a perfect plane,1112 aim to let omni platforms leave the polished factory floor.

Carry real weightactive

Adaptive and variable-geometry omni wheels, and coupled-drive layouts, push the payload ceiling so omnidirectional motion survives heavy industrial loads.910

Hold a headingactive

Because open-loop heading drifts several degrees over ten metres, closed-loop heading correction — measuring yaw and feeding a bias back into the mix — is a documented fix for heavy-duty omni robots,13 and the direct motivation for the IMU-fusion phase on this project.

Rethink the wheelactive

Alternatives sidestep mecanum's weaknesses entirely: spherical/ball drives for gapless omni-motion, active-caster and swerve modules that stay efficient by physically steering, and under-actuated coupled designs that get more directions from fewer motors.14 The "best" omni wheel is still an open question.
